## An Innovative Strategy for the Synthesis of Single-Atom Catalysts on MXene Surfaces

### Abstract
Two-dimensional transition metal carbide, Ti3C2Tx. exhibits excellent electrical conductivity and hydropnilicity, making it an ideal candidate for various electrochemical catalytic applications, particularly in water electrolysis. Also, MXene has been extensively investigated as a support material for single-atom catalysts (SACs) due to its ability to stabilize isolated metal atoms. However, conventional methods of synthesizing SACs on Ti3C2Tx often involve reducing atmospheres that can result in nanochemical instability, metal atom aggregation, and oxidation-persistent issues in the field. <br>
In this study, we present a novel approach to address these challenges by modifying the surface of Ti3C2Tx MXene through esterification with hemin, followed by the addition of melamine as spacer. We anticipate subsequent pyrolysis at approximately 600℃ facilitates the fixation of Fe atoms as single atoms through the condensation reaction of melaimine into g-C3N4. This method will not only enhances the stability of single-atom catalysts on the MXene surface but also protects them against oxidation, ensuring the catalysts remain active and durable during water electrolysis. Our research demonstrates that this surface modification strategy effectively prevents metal atom aggregation and oxidation, which are common drawbacks in MXene-based catalysts. The resulting Fe single-atom catalysts on MXene show promise for use in water electrolysis, potentially leading to more efficient and durable catalytic systems.
